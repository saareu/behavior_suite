# Subsystem 03 — Tracking Correction and Verification

**Status:** Gate 1 review draft  
**Scope level:** Product vision and subsystem boundary  
**Primary intended output:** `tracked_pose.parquet`  

## 1. Purpose

Subsystem 03 consumes a selected, completed Subsystem 02 pose-inference run and produces a tracking-corrected pose representation with substantially improved animal-identity continuity.

S3 is an automatic tracking-correction subsystem with a user review workspace. It is not merely a passive tracking viewer.

For validated current-lab experiments, S3 aims to correct the great majority of tracking failures automatically, with high precision and minimal required user effort.

Its intended primary pose output is:

```text
tracked_pose.parquet
```

The precise artifact contract and schema will be defined in a later design gate.

## 2. Position in the suite

The current conceptual sequence is:

```text
S1 — Video preprocessing and authoritative frame/timing mapping
S2 — SLEAP/SLEAP-NN pose inference, visual review, and pose-quality QC
S3 — Tracking correction, tracking verification, and tracking-related pose fixing
Later pose-finalization subsystem — imputation, smoothing, resampling, and related processing
```

S2 may perform provisional SLEAP tracking inside inference and may report pose-quality conditions relevant to later tracking. However, final biological identity assignment, final tracking verification, implanted/partner assignment, and identity-switch correction are downstream responsibilities.

S3 begins with those provisional pose and track assignments and takes responsibility for tracking correction and verification.

## 3. Core goal

S3 transforms provisional S2 pose and track assignments into corrected and internally consistent animal tracks.

When supported by the selected tracking profile, S3 may also establish experiment-specific identity roles, such as implanted mouse and partner mouse.

The subsystem should:

- correct tracking failures automatically where evidence is sufficiently strong;
- preserve the original S2 information and correction provenance;
- support fast visual review and acceptance;
- support limited manual correction;
- retain valuable hard examples for future pose-model improvement.

S3 does not claim universal or perfect tracking across arbitrary experimental settings.

## 4. MVP tracking profile

The first implementation targets the current laboratory setting:

- two mice;
- one mouse with a visually identifiable headstage;
- a known pose skeleton;
- prepared video generated by S1;
- pose output generated by S2;
- correction in the original prepared-video frame domain;
- no resampling.

This is a supported MVP profile, not the permanent definition of S3.

A profile identifier may later be defined, for example:

```text
current_lab_two_mouse_headstage_v1
```

The S3 architecture should remain open to future tracking backends or profiles, including alternative SLEAP tracking, global or bidirectional optimization, learned identity methods, IDTracker.ai, or other approaches.

## 5. S2 → S3 handoff

S3 consumes a selected completed S2 run.

The primary numeric input is `pose.parquet`. The associated prepared video is required for visual review.

S3 may additionally consume:

- `pose.slp` for native SLEAP interoperability;
- `pose_meta.json` for S2 pose-quality summaries;
- the S2 overlay as a review aid;
- referenced S1 sync and metadata artifacts for validation and provenance.

S3 must preserve the prepared-frame identity and timing contract inherited from S1 through S2. It must not independently reconstruct or redefine timing.

S2 QC information may help guide S3 processing or review, but an S2 QC warning is not by itself a confirmed tracking error.

## 6. Main responsibilities

### 6.1 Automatic tracking correction

S3 may correct:

- animal-identity switches;
- sustained identity reversal following a failed transition;
- incorrect implanted/partner assignment when the profile supports that distinction;
- duplicate or overlapping detections that interfere with tracking;
- incorrect headstage ownership;
- invalid track assignments;
- node assignment to the wrong track;
- implausible jumps or skeleton configurations that compromise tracking continuity;
- invalid nodes or instances that should be blanked.

This is a description of correction capabilities, not a required list of separate user-facing event types.

### 6.2 Tracking-related pose fixing

S3 may modify pose data when necessary to correct or preserve tracking.

Permitted conceptual actions include:

- swapping tracks;
- swapping tracks and blanking an invalid duplicate;
- moving an observed node between tracks;
- blanking an invalid node;
- blanking an invalid instance;
- restoring or reassigning an identifiable observed detection;
- applying limited profile-specific coordinate corrections required to preserve the validated behavior of the current tracking method.

S3 does not perform general gap filling, trajectory completion, smoothing, or pose reconstruction.

### 6.3 Visual verification and acceptance

S3 must provide an efficient visual workflow for reviewing the corrected result.

The user must be able to review and accept the result before it is treated as the selected S3 output.

The user is not expected to inspect every node, track, frame, or individual machine correction.

### 6.4 Manual correction

S3 should support a limited set of user correction actions for tracking errors that remain after automatic processing.

The exact MVP action set and interaction model will be defined during Gate 2.

### 6.5 Training-candidate capture

S3 MVP must allow the user to mark valuable frames or short intervals for future SLEAP model improvement.

Each saved candidate should retain:

- its prepared-frame location;
- useful neighboring-frame context;
- the user's reason or category;
- a reference to the source S2 run.

Automatic identification and prioritization of such candidates may be added conservatively, but are not required for the initial MVP.

S3 records this information but does not train or optimize the pose model.

## 7. Conservative automatic-correction policy

S3 automatic correction should prioritize precision and avoidance of harmful corrections.

For the current-lab profile, rules should be accepted only when they are supported by observed failure cases and do not introduce false corrections in the maintained regression examples.

When the evidence is insufficient, S3 should avoid speculative correction. More advanced unresolved-case detection may be introduced where it can be implemented conservatively without delaying the core lab workflow.

## 8. Current-code refactoring policy

The current tracking corrector contains valuable experimentally developed behavior but is not suitable for direct inclusion in its current structure.

The MVP should therefore perform a behavior-preserving structured rewrite.

The rewrite should improve:

- organization and naming;
- stage boundaries;
- correction-action handling;
- configuration;
- typed correction records;
- testability;
- provenance;
- maintainability;
- scientific readability.

Unusual thresholds or one-off conditions may remain when they encode validated current-lab behavior.

They should be named, documented, and configured where practical, but not generalized merely for stylistic consistency.

Apparently duplicated rules must not be removed until regression testing demonstrates that their removal preserves the intended behavior.

### Current-lab MVP compatibility constraint

The existing corrector is stateful and frame-order dependent.

During the initial refactor, its sequential behavior, rule ordering, and use of already-corrected preceding history must be preserved so that code cleanup does not reduce tracking efficacy.

This is a compatibility requirement of the initial current-lab backend, not a permanent S3 architectural requirement.

## 9. Correction records and user presentation

S3 may produce many detailed frame-level correction records.

These records should remain available for provenance, debugging, and validation. They must not automatically become separate review tasks shown to the user.

The UI must present tracking information in a digestible form. The exact event aggregation, transition detection, interval representation, and navigation model will be designed in Gate 2.

## 10. Frame and timing policy

S3 operates in the prepared-video frame domain inherited from S1 and S2.

The MVP does not resample pose data.

All corrections must preserve prepared-frame identity and associated S1 timing information.

Future profiles should preferably express temporal thresholds in time-aware terms and convert them using the source FPS, without requiring resampling.

## 11. MVP output direction

The intended primary output is:

```text
tracked_pose.parquet
```

It should represent the corrected tracked pose while preserving sufficient provenance to distinguish original S2 information from S3 modifications.

The exact parquet schema, supporting artifacts, correction-record representation, metadata files, and acceptance-state handling are deferred to a later contract-design gate.

S3 MVP is intended to run on a normal local PC and currently has no HPC requirement. No HPC-oriented job-manifest requirement is assumed at Gate 1.

## 12. Out of scope

S3 does not include:

- dense pose construction;
- resampling;
- general interpolation;
- Kalman or geometric imputation;
- long-gap reconstruction;
- smoothing;
- final behavior-analysis pose generation;
- behavior feature extraction or classification;
- SLEAP model training;
- SLEAP model optimization;
- execution of an active-learning workflow;
- universal support for arbitrary laboratories in the MVP.

Pose-finalization functions are deferred to a later subsystem, provisionally referred to as S4.

## 13. MVP success direction

S3 MVP should:

1. consume a validated, selected S2 run without modifying S1 or S2 artifacts;
2. preserve the effective behavior of the current-lab correction method;
3. automatically correct most observed current-lab tracking failures with high precision;
4. produce deterministic corrected tracking data;
5. preserve original pose information and correction provenance;
6. enable efficient visual review and explicit user acceptance;
7. require substantially less effort than manual tracking correction;
8. support limited manual pose/tracking fixes;
9. retain user-marked frames for future model improvement;
10. perform no resampling, imputation, or smoothing;
11. remain open to future tracking methods.

Quantitative accuracy and acceptance thresholds will be defined after representative regression datasets have been established.

## 14. Future direction

Potential future extensions include:

- additional tracking profiles and backends;
- non-sequential, global, or bidirectional identity optimization;
- automated localization of likely failure transitions;
- identity cues other than a headstage;
- learning from accumulated user corrections;
- automatic training-frame suggestion;
- integration with a future model-building workflow;
- efficient multi-session and batch review;
- generalization to different laboratories and experimental settings.

These future goals must not block delivery of the current-lab MVP.

## 15. Gate 1 decision status

This document is approved only after explicit user confirmation.

Gate 1 approval locks the product vision and subsystem boundary. It does not yet lock:

- the complete artifact set;
- the `tracked_pose.parquet` schema;
- the correction-record schema;
- the UI interaction design;
- the exact list of manual actions;
- the internal class/module architecture;
- quantitative tracking-performance thresholds;
- the choice of future alternative tracking backends.
